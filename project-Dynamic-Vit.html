Token-Pruned Dynamic ViT with LoRA Adaptation for Efficient On-Device Inference

Compression and fine-tuning of Vision Transformer (ViT) architectures for edge devices. The pipeline reduces the quadratic complexity of self-attention \(\mathcal{O}(N^2)\) by pruning non-informative tokens at intermediate layers 4, 7, and 10, while adapting weights via LoRA.

Authors: Gabriel FRANÇOIS, Léo LEROY, Avner EL BAZ (ENSAE 2026)
Datasets: ImageNet-1k, STL-10, CIFAR-100

Max Pruning at L10

91%

Only 9% of tokens retained at the final layer (keep_rate = 0.09).

Accuracy after LoRA Finetuning

~65%

Accuracy achieved quickly on the STL-10 test set.

FLOPs Reduction

-33%

The model goes from 0.18 GFLOPs (Teacher) to 0.12 GFLOPs (Pruned Student).

Low Parameter Size

1.56M

Reduced volume of parameters to fit edge constraints.

Image Patch Retention Profile by Layer

Self-Attention Mechanism & Adaptive Pruning

The base equation for MSA attention blocks is calculated over all patches of the cropped image:

\(z_{l}^{\prime}=MSA(LN(z_{l-1}))+z_{l-1}\)

To stabilize convergence and avoid early performance collapse, the pipeline deploys an adaptive sigmoidal pruning schedule that orchestrates the retention rate smoothly across epochs.
