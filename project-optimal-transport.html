Mathematical Formulation & Non-Local Regularization

To alleviate the rigidity of the strict coupling problem, the formulation introduces a relaxed transport polytope \(\mathcal{S}_{\kappa}\) weakening the classical marginal constraints[cite: 3]:

\(\mathcal{S}_{\kappa} = \{\Sigma \in [0,1]^{N \times N} \mid k_X 1 \le \Sigma 1 \le K_X 1, \; k_Y 1 \le \Sigma^T 1 \le K_Y 1, \; 1^T \Sigma 1 = M\}\)

The underlying geometry is incorporated by applying a discrete gradient operator \(G_X\) on the data connectivity graph, where the regularization energy under the \((p,q)\) norm is written as[cite: 3]:

\(I_{p,q}(G_X V) = \sum_{(i,j) \in E_X} \| w_{i,j}(V_i - V_j) \|_q^p\)

When \((p,q) = (1,1)\), the penalty corresponds to a non-smooth anisotropic Total Variation[cite: 3]. The global problem is then solved via a linear programming reformulation by introducing auxiliary variables bounding the geometric gradients[cite: 3].
